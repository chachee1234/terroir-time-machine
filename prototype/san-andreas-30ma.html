What is known and what is drawn

  • Known: the Pacific–Farallon ridge reached the North American trench about 28 million years ago; the Mendocino triple junction then moved north and the transform boundary lengthened behind it (Atwater 1970, GSA Bulletin; Atwater & Stock 1998, International Geology Review). The Pinnacles and Neenach volcanics are one field erupted about 23 million years ago, now about 315 km apart across the San Andreas (Matthews 1976, AAPG Bulletin). The Gulf of California began opening about 6 million years ago (Oskin & Stock 2003). Today the Pacific plate moves about 50 mm a year past North America, about 35 mm of it on the San Andreas. Relief: Terrain Tiles and ETOPO via AWS Open Data.
  • Drawn as a model: the ridge's shape and distance offshore, the triple junction moving at a steady speed, the sea-floor stripes (real magnetic stripes exist, these are spaced for clarity), the offset history (35 km per million years since 8 million years ago, 2 km before), where the volcanoes glow, the plate boundary drawn along today's San Andreas line for the whole film, colours.
