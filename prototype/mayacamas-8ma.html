What is measured and what is modelled

  • Measured: today's ground (USGS 3DEP via AWS Terrain Tiles); where the Sonoma Volcanics are (USGS SIM 2956, Graymer et al. 2007, units Ts*); that they erupted between about 8 and 2.5 million years ago, older to the south (Mankinen 1972, GSA Bulletin); fault traces and slip rates (USGS UCERF3 via the GEM active-faults database); today's satellite image (Copernicus Sentinel-2, 2026); the change from 41,000-year to 100,000-year ice-age cycles about a million years ago.
  • Modelled: when each patch of volcanic rock erupted (a smooth south-to-north ramp), how tall the volcanoes stood, how fast the ranges rose (slowly at first, faster after about 3.5 million years ago) and how valleys deepened, fault motion (today's rates, last 500,000 years only), the size of sea-level swings, all colours.
  • Why the ranges rose: the San Andreas plate boundary moved past this latitude about 10 to 8 million years ago, opening a gap under the plate that fed the volcanoes. Since about 5 to 3.5 million years ago the Pacific plate has pushed slightly toward North America as it slides past, squeezing the Coast Ranges up between strike-slip faults (Atwater 1970; Page et al. 1998, USGS).
